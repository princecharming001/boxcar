01 — The problem

Delivery is expensive because it moves one bag at a time.

A courier drives across the city, hands over a single package, and drives back. You pay for the whole trip. That's why same-day couriers start around twenty-five dollars, and why most things you'd like to send across town never get sent at all — the trip costs more than the thing.

The other half of the problem is what happens at the end of it. Your package gets handed to a stranger, then left on a step, and everyone involved just hopes it's still there when you get home.

02 — How it works

Three steps, and nobody carries anything to your door.

01

Drop it in

A shop or a sender loads your package into an open compartment. The door locks itself and is assigned to you.

02

It rides

The car runs one route across the metro carrying up to fifteen other deliveries. No handoffs, no sorting facility, no second truck.

03

You tap it open

You get a message with the curb location. Tap the link or scan the code on your door and your compartment opens. No app to download.

03 — The box

We don't build the car. We build the box.

Boxcar is hardware: a grid of steel compartments that mounts to a vehicle. It's the part of delivery that nobody has built yet — the piece between a car arriving and a package actually reaching the right person.

Sixteen compartments
Steel, each with its own electronic latch. One delivery per box.
One recipient per door
Your compartment is assigned when the package goes in and stays assigned until you open it.
Opens with your phone
A tap or a scan at the curb. No app, no code to remember, and every open is logged.
Temperature controlled
Each compartment is cooled independently, so groceries and cold items ride the same route as everything else.
Sealed end to end
Nobody's hands touch your package between the sender loading it and you opening it.

04 — Where this goes

The box doesn't care who's driving.

Today a trained driver runs each route. The grid is designed to mount on autonomous vehicles as they're licensed for public roads — the same steel, the same locks, the same tap to open.

That's deliberate. Nothing about your delivery changes when the driver goes away. What changes is the cost of the trip — and that's the part we're actually building toward. When crossing the city costs about a dollar, the things people never bother sending start moving.
