01 — The gap

Autonomy solved the driving. Nobody solved the handoff.

A robotaxi can already find your address and pull up to the curb. What it can't do is ring the doorbell, hand over a bag, or stop the wrong person from walking off with your order. Every autonomous delivery pitch quietly runs aground on that last thirty feet.

Boxcar builds the part that's missing: a grid of locked, temperature-controlled compartments that bolts onto whatever's driving. Not a vehicle — the layer between the vehicle and the person waiting for the box.

02 — The grid

We don't build cars. We build the box.

Nuro spent more than $2B building its own delivery vehicles before pivoting to licensing. We started where that ended: the hardware is the product, and it doesn't care what's underneath it.

/ 01 Sixteen locked doors Steel compartments, each with its own electronic latch. One delivery per box, assigned end to end — never pooled, never handled by a stranger.
/ 02 Opens for one phone The recipient taps a link or scans the QR on their door at the curb. Their compartment opens. No app to download, and every unlock is logged to a specific device.
/ 03 Temperature-controlled Active cooling per compartment, so the same grid moves documents, electronics, and anything that can't sit warm in a trunk across town.
/ 04 Vehicle-agnostic by design It mounts to a van today. It mounts to a licensed autonomous car tomorrow. Same steel, same latches, same unlock — the network grows with every vehicle that can carry it.

03 — Economics

Batching is the whole business.

Every courier app moves one bag per trip, which is why sending something across town costs more than the thing you're sending. Sixteen packages sharing one optimized route changes that math immediately — and changes it again when the driver goes away.
